# Sources for natural continuous-speech evaluation

Researched 15 October 2026. Ticket 2 still needs a reviewed 10-20 minute evaluation corpus. This investigation identified sources and inspected AMI's official annotation archive. No meeting audio was downloaded or listened to, and no evaluation dataset was accepted.

## Recommendation

Start with the **AMI Meeting Corpus**, preferably genuine non-scenario meetings. It offers original continuous recordings, speaker metadata, transcripts, and estimated word timings under a clear public license. Select intact stretches totaling 10-20 minutes, preserve ordinary speech and pauses, and manually review keyword boundaries. This is a project recommendation based on the verified source properties below.

The requirement remains pending until the selected recordings satisfy [our collection and acceptance rules](../../docs/data/README.md). Existing public audio can reduce collection work, but downloading a corpus alone does not establish annotation completeness, all-six-keyword coverage, or speaker separation.

## AMI, best fit

AMI has 100 hours of meeting recordings. About two-thirds are elicited design-team role-play; the remainder are naturally occurring meetings. Signals and transcripts use CC BY 4.0. Choose the genuine meetings deliberately rather than describing every AMI recording as spontaneous. [Official corpus and license](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/)

The non-scenario catalog distinguishes actual colleague discussions from other elicited tasks. Examples include IN1002, a research discussion, and IN1007, a discussion of keyword spotting. These are candidate sources, not confirmed six-keyword selections. [Meeting descriptions](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/nonscenariomeetings.shtml)

The transcripts were prepared and checked by people. **Word and phoneme timings were generated by forced alignment**, so they are estimates, not manually verified word boundaries. Speaker channels are identified. Review every target occurrence against the audio before event scoring. [Transcription process](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/transcription.shtml)

Download the [manual annotations v1.6.2 ZIP](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/AMICorpusAnnotations/ami_public_manual_1.6.2.zip), listed as 22 MB. The [official download chooser](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/download/) permits individual meetings and streams. It lists a headset mix as one WAV, averaging about 30 MB per meeting, or individual headsets at about 120 MB per meeting. Its manual-annotation package label does not imply manually placed word timings.

AMI provides 16 kHz WAV recordings. Its metadata includes participant/channel mappings in `corpusResources/meetings.xml`. Preserve the global participant identities when assigning validation and test; a channel letter alone is not a person identifier across the corpus. [Signal formats and mappings](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/signals.shtml)

AMI documents its ethics procedures and provides the blank consent form signed by participants. Record this publisher evidence in provenance; we do not possess individual signed forms. [Ethics and consent](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/ethicsandconsent.shtml)

Meeting speech includes overlap. Our current event CSV rejects overlapping rows, so select and review stretches compatible with that rule. Listening is also needed to identify hard negatives and usable silence; transcript gaps do not automatically prove either.

## Concrete candidate found in official annotations

Downloaded only the [manual annotation archive](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/AMICorpusAnnotations/ami_public_manual_1.6.2.zip), about 22 MB, into ignored `data/research_ami/`. Its SHA-256 is `b56e5babb2496b8795deeeda7e71178d7fbc9963f94276cf2a3f4b56ebbc9f9d`. These are our measured metadata findings, not claims from listening or a model Evaluation Run.

The archive contains 687 `*.words.xml` files. Their word elements expose `starttime` and `endtime`. The meeting metadata maps local `nxt_agent` letters to global participant IDs with `global_name`. Across meetings marked `type="nonscenario"`, counting case-insensitive exact target tokens with positive-length timestamps, excluding truncated tokens, gave:

| Keyword | Annotation occurrences |
| --- | ---: |
| left | 45 |
| right | 1,478 |
| up | 461 |
| down | 114 |
| go | 408 |
| stop | 27 |

One intact candidate is **IN1002, 482.14-1082.14 seconds**, or **08:02.14-18:02.14**. Its annotations contain `left: 1`, `right: 6`, `up: 4`, `down: 1`, `go: 3`, `stop: 1` across all speaker channels. IN1002 is marked as a non-scenario speech-research discussion. Its global participants are `MIO076`, `MIO022`, `MIO050`, and `MIO078`.

These counts cover the full meeting mix, not one individual headset. A single speaker's headset cannot inherit all channels' annotations unchanged. Download the meeting mix for an all-speaker evaluation, or match a selected headset to its own annotations and account for audible cross-talk. Any crop must shift times by its start offset. This candidate has sparse examples for several classes; one occurrence is insufficient for a strong per-class reliability claim. It still needs audio review, and a separate validation/test selection with no shared global participants. Another meeting ID alone does not establish different people.

## Alternatives and limitations

| Source | Verified properties | Fit for this ticket |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/12) | CC BY 4.0, 16 kHz read audiobook speech, carefully segmented and aligned. dev-clean is 337 MB; test-clean is 346 MB. | Useful connected read speech, but released utterance clips are not spontaneous long recordings. Do not concatenate them to fabricate continuous recordings. |
| [GigaSpeech](https://github.com/SpeechColab/GigaSpeech) | Official metadata retains original-source URLs and segment begin/end times. Its example has `speaker: N/A`. | Requires word alignment and verified speaker identification. Repository Apache licensing alone should not be treated as proof of uniform permission for all source audio. Check actual access terms. |
| [MSWC](https://mlcommons.org/datasets/multilingual-spoken-words/) | CC BY 4.0 word corpus with downloadable alignments. | Its one-second word clips do not meet continuous-recording requirements. Originals come from Common Voice sentences, rather than long conversations. |
| [Common Voice](https://github.com/common-voice/common-voice) | Speech donations for public-domain speech datasets. [Mozilla distinguishes scripted sentence reading from spontaneous speech](https://community.mozilladatacollective.com/commonvoice-27/). | Do not assume the scripted corpus is long spontaneous audio or has word timings. Check the exact English release, annotation fields, access rules, and durations before selecting it. |
| [TED-LIUM 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04699) | Publisher-authored paper describes 452 hours of transcribed English TED speech. | Talks are prepared presentations. The historical [OpenSLR 51 page](https://www.openslr.org/51/) returned “Resource not found” during this check, and the LIUM host timed out. Download availability and archive terms were not confirmed, so this is not the first choice. |

LibriSpeech can use local [Montreal Forced Aligner alignment](https://montreal-forced-aligner.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/workflows/alignment.html), followed by manual correction. A third-party [LibriSpeech alignment repository](https://github.com/CorentinJ/librispeech-alignments) exists, but separate annotation licensing was not confirmed. Generating local alignments from permitted originals avoids depending on unverified annotation permissions. Forced alignment proposes timings from supplied text; it does not replace listening.

## Acceptance steps for AMI

1. Extend the annotation inspection above to select both partitions. Both validation and test need all six, plus reviewed negatives and silence. The IN1002 candidate alone does not complete this.
2. Assign different global speakers and source sessions to validation and test before threshold tuning. Another corpus has different identifiers, but this cannot prove no real person also contributed to anonymous Speech Commands.
3. Download only selected audio. Keep original recordings; crop intact stretches with documented offsets and no splicing.
4. Convert to supported mono integer PCM WAV, total 600-1200 seconds, at most 10 minutes per recording under our current contract.
5. Review all keyword occurrences, hard negatives and silence. Shift source times by crop offsets, then export `recording_id,keyword,start_s,end_s`.
6. Record source version, CC BY attribution, consent evidence, participant mappings, source groups, durations, checksums and split decisions. Run the existing validator and have a second listener check boundaries before accepting the corpus.

If AMI cannot cover the required vocabulary and suitable negatives within this duration without excessive overlap, keep the requirement pending and record consenting volunteers using the existing instructions.
